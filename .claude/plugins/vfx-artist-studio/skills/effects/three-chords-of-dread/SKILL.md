# Three Chords of Dread -- Temporal Frequency Layering for Volumetric Depth

Reference: Sunn O))) -- three sustained notes that sum into a wall of sound. Each note
operates at a different frequency, a different timescale, a different emotional register.
The drone is geological. The churn is atmospheric. The spark is electrical. Separately,
they are simple. Together, they are dread.

The three chords are not three separate effects. They are three temporal frequency layers
that sum (never multiply) into a single density and emission field. The viewer perceives
one thing with depth, not three things stacked.

> "Three chords is all you need. But they must be the right three."

---

## Principle

Volumetric motion without temporal frequency layering reads as a single physical process.
Real industrial plumes contain simultaneous motions at radically different timescales:
the macro drift of the entire plume body (minutes), the rolling churn of turbulent eddies
(seconds), and the fast crackle of combustion emission (sub-second). These three layers
sum to produce the perceptual depth that makes a plume feel real.

**Chord 1 -- DRONE:** The HYSPLIT transport shape. Glacial, massive, slow. The entire
plume drifts at 0.001-0.01 units/frame. This is the fundamental note.

**Chord 2 -- CHURN:** Curl noise advection. Fluid, rolling, mid-frequency. Turbulent
eddies displace the density at 0.05-0.2 units/frame, 4-6 octaves. This is the body.

**Chord 3 -- SPARK:** Curvature-driven emission crackle. Fast, stochastic, high-frequency.
Emission flickers at 0.5-2.0 units/frame. This is the edge.

The chords are summed, not multiplied. Multiplication causes one chord to gate another,
destroying the independence of the layers. Summation preserves each chord's contribution
at all times.

---

## Procedure

### Step 1 -- Establish Chord 1: DRONE

The drone is the base transport shape from HYSPLIT or the Gaussian plume model. It moves
glacially -- the viewer barely perceives its drift across frames. The drone defines the
macro silhouette: where the plume IS, at the largest scale.

```python
import numpy as np
from oco_viz.plume.turbulent import apply_turbulence

# Chord 1: DRONE -- base plume shape with glacial drift
# The base density field from Gaussian or HYSPLIT transport
# drift_rate: 0.001-0.01 units/frame (barely perceptible)
drone_density = base_density.copy()

# Apply glacial drift via coordinate translation per frame
drift_offset = drone_drift_rate * time_index  # default 0.005 units/frame
# Shift is applied via coordinate offset in the plume generation stage
```

The drone is not generated by this skill -- it comes from the upstream plume pipeline.
This skill assumes `drone_density` is provided as input, already containing the base
transport shape with its inherent slow drift.

### Step 2 -- Apply Chord 2: CHURN

The churn is curl noise advection applied to the drone density. It creates rolling
turbulent motion at a medium timescale -- visible as eddies, billows, and internal
circulation. The churn displaces the drone density without altering the macro silhouette.

```python
from oco_viz.plume.noise import curl_noise_3d

# Chord 2: CHURN -- curl noise displacement at mid-frequency
# Speed: 0.05-0.2 units/frame (visible rolling motion)
curl_dx, curl_dy, curl_dz = curl_noise_3d(
    shape=drone_density.shape,
    octaves=churn_octaves,       # default 5, range 4-6
    lacunarity=2.0,
    gain=0.5,
    seed=churn_seed + int(time_index * churn_speed * 100),  # temporal evolution
)

# Displace drone density coordinates by curl field
from scipy.ndimage import map_coordinates

nz, ny, nx = drone_density.shape
coords_z, coords_y, coords_x = np.mgrid[0:nz, 0:ny, 0:nx]
disp_scale = churn_strength * min(nz, ny, nx) * 0.3  # default churn_strength=0.15

coords_z_d = coords_z.astype(np.float64) + curl_dz.astype(np.float64) * disp_scale
coords_y_d = coords_y.astype(np.float64) + curl_dy.astype(np.float64) * disp_scale
coords_x_d = coords_x.astype(np.float64) + curl_dx.astype(np.float64) * disp_scale

churn_density = map_coordinates(
    drone_density.astype(np.float64),
    [coords_z_d, coords_y_d, coords_x_d],
    order=1,
    mode="constant",
    cval=0.0,
).astype(np.float32)
```

### Step 3 -- Sum Chords 1 and 2 for Final Density

The final density is the churn-displaced drone density. The churn does not add density --
it redistributes the drone density through advection. The summation is implicit in the
displacement operation.

```python
# Final density = churn-displaced drone
# (The churn is a displacement, not an additive field,
#  so the "sum" is the displaced field itself)
final_density = churn_density
```

### Step 4 -- Compute Chord 3: SPARK

The spark is curvature-driven emission from the `curvature-driven-emission` technique.
It operates on the final density at the highest temporal frequency -- fast flicker that
reads as combustion crackle at the plume edges.

```python
# Chord 3: SPARK -- emission crackle at high frequency
# Speed: 0.5-2.0 units/frame (fast flicker)
# Uses curvature-driven-emission technique on final_density

# Compute vorticity proxy
curl_dx_s, curl_dy_s, curl_dz_s = curl_noise_3d(
    shape=final_density.shape,
    octaves=4,
    seed=spark_seed + int(time_index * spark_speed * 100),  # fast temporal evolution
)
vorticity_mag = np.sqrt(
    curl_dx_s**2 + curl_dy_s**2 + curl_dz_s**2
).astype(np.float32)
v_max = vorticity_mag.max()
if v_max > 0:
    vorticity_mag /= v_max

# Compute curvature from final_density
gz, gy, gx = np.gradient(final_density.astype(np.float64))
grad_mag = np.sqrt(gz**2 + gy**2 + gx**2)
grad_safe = np.maximum(grad_mag, 1e-8)
curv = np.abs(
    np.gradient(gx / grad_safe, axis=2)
    + np.gradient(gy / grad_safe, axis=1)
    + np.gradient(gz / grad_safe, axis=0)
).astype(np.float32)
c_max = curv.max()
if c_max > 0:
    curv /= c_max

# Oxygen proxy and emission
oxygen = np.maximum(vorticity_mag, curv * 0.5)
import math
pulse = 1.0 + 0.2 * (
    math.sin(2.0 * math.pi * 0.2 * time_index * spark_speed)
    * math.sin(2.0 * math.pi * 0.05 * time_index * spark_speed)
)
final_emission = final_density * oxygen * spark_intensity * pulse
np.maximum(final_emission, 0.0, out=final_emission)
```

### Step 5 -- Verify Three-Chord Lock

The lock criterion: each chord must be wedgeable independently, but when all three play
together, they must read as "one thing with depth" -- not three separate effects.

**Verification process:**
1. Render drone only (chord 1 solo) -- should read as a static, massive shape
2. Render drone + churn (chords 1+2) -- should add rolling life without changing silhouette
3. Render all three (chords 1+2+3) -- should add edge crackle; the three together should
   feel like a single plume with physical depth
4. If any chord is independently identifiable as a separate layer, the balance is wrong

### Composition Rule

```
final_density  = churn_displacement(drone_density)
final_emission = spark_emission(final_density, velocity)
```

Density chords sum via displacement (churn reshapes drone). Emission chord sums additively
onto the resulting density field. The three layers never multiply.

## Anti-Patterns

### 1. The Multiplied Chords

**Symptom:** When one chord is at a local minimum (near zero), the entire output drops
to near zero. The plume blinks in and out as the chords gate each other. Motion appears
to have synchronized pauses.

**Cause:** Multiplying chords instead of summing them. `drone * churn * spark` means a
zero in any chord kills the product. The chords lose their independence.

**Fix:** Sum, never multiply. `final_density = churn_displacement(drone_density)`.
`final_emission = spark_emission(final_density)`. Each chord's contribution is preserved
regardless of the others' instantaneous values.

### 2. The Synchronized Frequencies

**Symptom:** All three motion layers move at the same apparent speed. The plume has only
one perceived timescale -- it churns uniformly. There is no depth of motion, no sense of
scale, no geological vs atmospheric vs electrical contrast.

**Cause:** Setting drone, churn, and spark speeds to similar values. If
`drone_drift_rate` = 0.1, `churn_speed` = 0.15, and `spark_speed` = 0.2, the three are
barely distinguishable.

**Fix:** Ensure at least 10x speed ratio between adjacent chords. Drone: 0.005.
Churn: 0.1 (20x faster). Spark: 1.0 (10x faster than churn). The frequency separation
IS the perceptual depth.

### 3. The Missing Chord

**Symptom:** The plume has motion but lacks depth. It reads as a single physical process
at one timescale. Something feels flat but is hard to articulate.

**Cause:** One of the three chords is disabled or set to negligible strength. Most
commonly, the drone is missing (no macro shape) or the spark is missing (no edge crackle).

**Fix:** All three chords must be active at Exhibition tier. Render each chord in
isolation to verify its contribution. If a chord is not visually distinguishable in solo,
increase its strength. If it IS distinguishable, it is contributing.

### 4. The Identifiable Layer

**Symptom:** A reviewer can point at the screen and say "that is the churn layer" or
"that is the spark." The layers are visible as separate effects rather than as a unified
phenomenon with depth.

**Cause:** One chord's parameters are too extreme relative to the others. Typically,
`churn_strength` is too high, creating visible eddies that read as a second plume
overlaid on the first.

**Fix:** Reduce the dominant chord's strength until the three read as one. The lock
criterion is: "one thing with depth." If an untrained viewer perceives three things, the
balance is wrong.

- [ ] Three distinct temporal frequencies active: drone (slow), churn (mid), spark (fast)
- [ ] At least 10x speed ratio between adjacent frequency layers
- [ ] Chords sum, never multiply
- [ ] Drone provides macro silhouette and glacial drift

- [ ] Spark provides edge emission crackle with arrhythmic pulse
